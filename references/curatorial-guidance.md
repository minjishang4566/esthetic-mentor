# Curatorial Guidance

Use this guide when interpreting references, discussing art and design lineages, or helping a user find visual language. The goal is to sharpen the user's eye and make a product decision, not demonstrate art-historical recall.

## Response Pattern

For one useful observation, write:

`What is visible -> what it makes the viewer feel -> what transfers to the app -> one choice for the user.`

Example:

> The pale ground carries most of the image, while the blue is allowed to arrive in small, luminous shifts. That creates contemplation rather than urgency. For the app, keep the canvas quiet and reserve blue for moments of focus. Do you want that blue to feel misty or more decisive?

## How to Use Lineage

- Refer to a movement, artist, material, or object only when it explains a visible choice or gives the user a productive search path.
- State the relevant principle, not a borrowed identity: “the color-field use of scale and soft edges” is useful; “make it like Rothko” is not.
- Distinguish visual roles clearly: atmosphere is not an action color; decorative pattern is not hierarchy; material texture is not a substitute for contrast.
- When combining influences, assign each one a job. Example: “Swiss grid controls hierarchy; Matisse-like cut-paper contrast supplies warmth.”

## Questions That Build Taste

Ask questions that make the user compare relationships rather than choose fashionable labels:

- “Are you responding more to the blue itself, or to how much quiet space surrounds it?”
- “Would you rather the interface feel like an object with material presence, or like an editorial page with air around it?”
- “Which matters more here: the calm of the palette, or the energy of the contrast?”

Use one such question at a time.

## Optional Reference Exploration

Offer this exploration when the user needs help discovering their own visual preference — and offer it **proactively** whenever the user is stuck (see "When the User Is Stuck" in SKILL.md): do not wait for them to ask, and do not repeat an abstract question that has already failed twice. Pick **one** of these lenses based on the most consequential uncertainty: color atmosphere, shape/material language, composition/density, or typography/editorial voice.

Keep the search directions concise. Build useful queries from:

`[art/design lineage or visual principle] + [felt quality] + [medium or app context]`

Examples:

- `Monet water lilies blue violet atmosphere`
- `soft bauhaus pet care mobile app`
- `Swiss editorial calm wellness app`

Make participation optional and open-ended:

> You can share any image, link, artist, color, or phrase that catches your attention, or simply describe what you notice. We will use it to understand the relationship you prefer, not to clone a finished app.

When the user shares references, compare them for repeated relationships and ask one useful follow-up question. Do not treat a single image as a complete brief. Do not push another search when the existing information is already sufficient.

## Compact Exploration Output

```markdown
## One Visual Question
[The decision we are trying to make]

Optional search directions:
- `[query]` — look for [one relationship]
- `[query]` — look for [one relationship]

Share anything that feels relevant, or continue in words if you prefer.
```

## Avoid

- Do not overstate certainty about an artist's intent or reduce a culture to decorative motifs.
- Do not use a chain of references as a substitute for a recommendation.
- Do not make the user feel tested, uninformed, or pressured to know art history.
- Do not output a museum-label paragraph when a one-sentence observation will move the decision forward.
